This paper estimates how much of India's May 2026 gold import duty hike, from 6 to 15 percent, passed through to domestic gold prices. Exploiting the sharp and unanticipated policy discontinuity as a quasi-experiment, this paper applies an interrupted time series design with Newey-West heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation-consistent standard errors to 1,160 daily observations from the India Bullion and Jewellers Association benchmark price series spanning January 2022 through June 2026.

The duty hike raised the domestic gold premium by Rs.\,9,743 per 10 grams, representing 79.4 percent of the theoretical ceiling, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 69.9 to 88.8 percent. Full pass-through is formally rejected ($t = -4.30$, $p < 0.001$). Local projections confirm the adjustment was immediate on the first trading day and held on a stable plateau through the fourteenth day, with no evidence of anticipatory pricing. An ARDL bounds test confirms cointegration between domestic and international gold prices in the pre-hike period, establishing the shift as permanent rather than transitory. Three independent counterfactual methods converge within 5 percent of the primary estimate, and fake-date placebo tests confirm the result is specific to May 13, 2026.

This paper also documents asymmetric price transmission: the symmetric July 2024 duty cut passed through within one week, while the hike remained incomplete after 25 trading days. The incomplete pass-through is consistent with supply-side dampening from front-loaded inventory imported at the prior duty rate, seasonal demand weakness, and historical smuggling elasticity.

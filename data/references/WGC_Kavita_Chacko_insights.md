## Key Insights by Theme

### Theme 1: Pre-Treatment Confounds (CRITICAL — affects ITS design)

The weeks before May 13, 2026 were NOT a clean baseline. Multiple non-duty policy actions were already constraining supply and affecting premiums:

**A. UAE Supply Channel Disruption (March–May 2026)**
- Source: March 2026 update
- US-Israel strikes on Iran disrupted flight routes from the UAE (India's key supply hub: 24% of gold imports in 2025)
- Effect: Domestic market briefly moved to near-premium in early March before reverting to discount
- Scale: March 2026 discounts averaged US$46/oz (vs US$15/oz in February)
- Imports in March 2026 fell to a 9-month low: US$3.1bn (~20–25t, vs 12-month avg of 62t)

**B. Banks' IGST Pause (April–May 2026)**
- Source: May 2026 update (Footnote 7), April 2026 update
- Banks halted bullion imports for over a month awaiting IGST exemption notification renewal
- Tightened domestic supply even before the duty hike
- Resolved just before May 13

**C. Administrative Tightening (April 2026)**
- Source: April 2026 update (Footnote 5)
- April 2, 2026: Restrictions imposed on imports of gold, silver, platinum jewellery and platinum alloys containing >1% gold by weight
- Purpose: Curb FTA misuse (particularly UAE CEPA route)
- Effect: Discounts narrowed from US$46/oz to ~US$8/oz in first two weeks of April

**D. Front-Loading Before Hike (April 2026)**
- Source: May 2026 update
- April 2026 imports: US$5.6bn, up >80% y/y — estimated 48–55t
- Refiners increased doré intake around Akshaya Tritiya (April 19–20)
- Anecdotal evidence of front-loading in anticipation of duty hike
- Effect: Ample supply of old-duty inventory IMMEDIATELY post-May 13 — structurally suppresses post-hike premium

**Paper implication:** Add supply-disruption control dummy (approx. March 1–May 12, 2026) in Notebook 05 robustness. Show β₁ is stable with and without it.

### Theme 2: Seasonality as Structural Downward Bias on Pass-Through

- Source: May 2026 update
- **Mid-May to mid-June is inauspicious for gold buying** in India (the entire post-hike window in our dataset)
- WGC explicitly cites seasonal weakness as limiting pass-through: *"the increase came at a time of seasonally weak demand — summer wedding purchases are largely over, and the period from mid-May to mid-June is considered inauspicious for buying gold"*
- Historical pattern: June–July is always the off-season (June and July 2025 updates confirm this annually)

**Paper implication:** Our pass-through estimate is a **lower bound**. Explicitly state this in Section 5 (Results). Had the hike occurred in October (Diwali) or February (wedding season), pass-through would be higher. This is actually a strong finding — even at the seasonally weakest moment, 60%+ passed through on Day 1.

---

### Theme 3: Front-Loading Predicts Rising Pass-Through Over Time

- Source: May 2026 update + market logic
- Dealers held large inventory at 6% duty immediately post-hike
- As old inventory depletes, new imports at 15% duty dominate supply
- WGC: *"bullion dealers likely offloaded inventory imported at lower duty rates, adding to market supply"*

**Paper implication:** Local Projections (Notebook 03) should show rising β_h over h=1..30. We now have a mechanism-based prediction: premium should trend upward as inventory depletes. The 20-day upward trend already visible (Day 1: +7,872 → Day 20: +11,457) is consistent with this.

---

### Theme 4: Smuggling Creates a Structural Ceiling on Pass-Through

- Source: May 2026 update
- Correlation between duty rate and unofficial imports: **+0.52** (2013–2026)
- After 2013 hike: unofficial imports rose from ~10t (Q1 2013) to 70t (Q1 2014) — 7× in under a year
- After July 2024 cut to 6%: unofficial imports fell to near zero
- After July 2022 hike to 15%: unofficial imports rose from 17t to ~50t by late 2022

**Paper implication:** 100% pass-through is theoretically impossible — as domestic premium approaches the theoretical ceiling (~₹13,000/10g), smuggling becomes profitable, adding supply and compressing the premium. State this as the structural ceiling mechanism in Section 5 Discussion. Sets up Paper 2.

### Theme 5: Customs Notified Value vs Spot Price (Parity Caveat)

- Source: March 2026 update (Footnote 3)
- *"Customs duty on gold imports is levied on a notified value fixed by CBIC, rather than solely on invoice prices. There is no fixed schedule for revising the notified value. It is typically updated every few weeks and more frequently during periods when international price volatility is high."*

**Paper implication:** Our parity formula (Gold_USD × FX × duty%) is an approximation of the actual import cost. The true landed price uses CBIC notified value, which can lag or lead spot. This explains some noise in the domestic_premium series during high-volatility periods. Worth a footnote in Section 3 (Data).

### Theme 6: FX Channel Evidence

- Source: March 2026 update
- February 2026: domestic prices fell 3.5% while international prices rose 5% — driven by INR appreciation (stronger rupee makes imports cheaper) and fewer CBIC notified value revisions
- This is a clean real-world demonstration of the FX channel operating in our study period

**Paper implication:** Strengthens Section 5b (FX Stabilization Channel). The FX channel is bidirectional — INR appreciation reduces imported gold costs; INR depreciation raises them independently of duty.

---

### Theme 7: July 2024 Duty Cut — The Symmetric Event

- Source: July 2024 and August 2024 updates
- July 24, 2024: Duty cut from 15% → 6% in Union Budget
- Immediate effect: domestic prices fell ~6% m/m; y-t-d gains went from 16% to 10%
- Market response: *"strong demand pushed the domestic gold price to a premium relative to the landed price following five months of discounts"*
- WGC estimated the cut would add at least 50t to demand in H2 2024

**Paper implication:** The July 2024 cut is the symmetric event to May 2026. If we had enough pre/post data around July 2024, we could run a symmetric ITS and compare pass-through speed and magnitude in both directions. This is an excellent Paper 2 or extended robustness angle. Our dataset covers July 2024 with complete Yahoo Finance data (IBJA has a gap Jul 16–Oct 31, 2024 but that is a server gap, MCAR).

### Theme 8: Premium vs Discount Regime Dynamics

Across all articles, WGC tracks NCDEX premium/discount vs international price. Key pattern from the articles that aligns with our dataset sub-periods:

| Period | Market State | WGC characterization |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|
| 2022–Jul 2024 | High duty (15%) | Generally positive premium over landed (international + 15%) |
| Jul 2024 – Feb 2026 | Low duty (6%) | Briefly premium after cut, then mostly at parity or slight discount |
| Feb–May 12 2026 | Pre-hike confound period | Deep discounts (US$15–58/oz) due to UAE disruption + bank pause |
| May 13, 2026+ | Post-hike | Discount to 15% parity (~US$150–164/oz) but premium to 6% baseline |

Our domestic_premium uses **6% as the fixed baseline**, so the post-hike period shows a large positive premium. WGC uses **current landed price** as reference, so they see a discount. These are complementary — not contradictory.

### Theme 9: Seasonal Calendar (useful for EDA and robustness)

From multiple articles:
- **Inauspicious/off-season:** Mid-May to mid-August; mid-December to mid-January
- **Wedding/festive season:** November–March (peak); regional festivals year-round
- **Akshaya Tritiya:** April (major buying occasion — Apr 19–20 in 2026)
- **Diwali season:** October–November
- **Financial year-end (March):** Demand suppression (liquidity constraints, retailer inventory liquidation)

## Impact on Research Design

### What stays the same
- ITS + Local Projections + ARDL — core design unchanged
- May 13, 2026 as the treatment date
- domestic_premium (IBJA PM − 6% parity) as the outcome variable

### What gets added / strengthened

1. **Section 2 (Policy Background):** Expand beyond the gazette notifications to cover the full policy package: UAE CEPA tightening (Notification 18), advance authorization cap (100 kg), bank IGST pause, PM's consumer appeal. The duty hike was part of a multi-tool intervention.

2. **Section 3 (Data):** Add footnote on CBIC notified value vs spot price as source of parity approximation error.

3. **Section 5 (Results):** Explicitly frame pass-through estimate as a lower bound — two independent downward biases: (a) seasonal inauspicious period, (b) old duty inventory front-loading.

4. **Section 6 (Discussion):** Smuggling elasticity (+0.52) as the structural ceiling on pass-through. Rising β_h in Local Projections = inventory depletion signature.

5. **Notebook 05 (Robustness):** Add pre-treatment supply-disruption dummy (March 1–May 12, 2026) as an additional robustness check.

6. **Paper 2 setup:** Symmetric event analysis (July 2024 cut vs May 2026 hike). Smuggling response data from DGCI&S (available September 2026).
